Detailed Description

This layout keeps its own sequence of children, independent of the attached container's own child list (which only ever determines Z-order/hit-testing): built up via this layout's own add_child() (appends to the end) or insert_child() (anywhere); a child added directly via Container::add_child() instead isn't part of this sequence, so it's simply left wherever it already is, unarranged by this layout.

Arranges that sequence one after another along a single axis (see Orientation), separated by a fixed get_gap(), within a fixed get_padding() kept around the whole sequence. This divides the container into a sequence of cells, one per child: along the main axis, a cell is either exactly as big as its own child's effective preferred size (a stretch factor of 0 or less) or a share of whatever space is left over once every such fixed-size child's cell has been accounted for, proportional to its own stretch factor relative to the other stretched children's (every cell then just splits the main axis evenly, unless some are explicitly pinned to their own preferred size via 0 instead), except that a stretched child whose plain share would undercut its own effective minimum size instead gets exactly that, with the leftover redistributed among the remaining stretched children the same way, repeated until none are left undercut; along the perpendicular (cross) axis, every cell is simply the container's own full extent there (minus padding).

How a child actually sits within its own cell (possibly larger than its own preferred size: filled, shrunk-and-aligned near/far, or shrunk-and-centered) is then up to its own anchor (the same flags DefaultLayout reads for the other purpose of reacting to a resize), independently per axis: Left or Top alone (whichever applies to that axis; the default anchor) aligns it near, at its own effective preferred size; Right or Bottom alone aligns it far instead, same size; neither aligns it centered; both together fills the cell, overriding its own preferred size. A stretched child (the default) whose anchor doesn't fill the main axis this way just sits somewhere within its own, larger cell instead of actually growing: stretch and anchor are independent, exactly as they already are for the cross axis. A plain stack of fixed-size children, each kept at its own preferred size regardless of this layout's own size, therefore needs 0 explicitly set on every one of them, not just left at the default.

None of this ever shrinks a child past its own effective minimum size, though: once a cell (on either axis) becomes smaller than that, the child keeps its minimum size instead and simply overflows the cell, and possibly this layout's own bounds, rather than shrinking further or distorting its content.

Each child's own effective preferred/minimum size is re-queried live on every perform() call, not cached here: it can genuinely change over time (e.g., faces::Label's, after its text changes). Any face type whose own calculation is actually expensive to repeat is expected to cache that internally instead, where the relevant inputs (and when they've changed) are actually known.

Also reports its own preferred/minimum size (see Layout): the sum of every child's own preferred/minimum size along the main axis (plus gaps/padding), and their max along the cross axis (plus padding), ignoring stretch/anchor entirely: that's the size this sequence would occupy if every child actually got exactly its own preferred/minimum size, which is what a Container with this layout attached reports as its own effective size.

Returns the smallest this sequence could ever actually be laid out at without overflowing, along this layout's own main axis (plus the fixed gaps between children, plus this layout's own padding on that axis): every non-stretched (stretch <= 0) child's own effective preferred size (that's what perform() always gives it, never less), plus every stretched child's own effective minimum size instead (the only ones perform() can actually shrink that far). Along the cross axis: the largest of every child's own effective minimum size there (plus padding), same as before, since the cross axis has no stretch distinction to begin with.

◆ set_stretch()

void fl7::gui::layout::BoxLayout::set_stretch ( const Face & child,
float stretch )

Sets the given child's stretch factor: 0 or less means its cell is exactly its own get_effective_preferred_size() along this layout's own axis, not participating in the distribution at all; a positive value (1, the default for every child until told otherwise) means its cell instead takes that share of whatever space is left over once every non-stretched (stretch <= 0) child's cell has taken its own preferred size, relative to the other stretched children's own factors (e.g., two children both left at the default 1 split the leftover space evenly; one set to 2 and another left at 1 split it two-to-one). Either way, purely about the cell's own size, independent of whether the child actually fills it (see this class's own doc comment: that's its anchor's call, not this one's).
